# Eye closure from the eyelid distance: first round, waiting for the human spot check (2026-10-05, revised)

First round agreed with Kaiwen on 2026-10-04: judge closure from the distance between the upper and the lower eyelid
point, run it on the videos, then spot-check part of the result by eye. The finer classes (blink / closed / squint) come
after this round. **No conclusion yet: the spot check has not been done.**

**Basic information.** Videos 0-12. Model of a video = the model at its plateau point (plateau at 0 labels: the
20-label model), ResNet-50, final snapshot, whole-video prediction, no confidence cut-off. Script:
`../../scripts/blink_by_eyelid_distance.py` (formulas in its header).

Rule (revised 2026-10-05): eye opening `O = y(eyelid_bottom) - y(eyelid_top)`; relative opening `r = O / median of O over
the video`. A frame is **untrusted** when the eyelid points are implausible: O < 0 (upper point below the lower one), the
two points more than 0.3 eye widths apart horizontally, or either eyelid confidence < 0.3. A **closure frame** is a
trusted frame with r < 0.70; closure frames less than 5 frames (83 ms) apart form one event, kept only if at least 3
frames (50 ms) long. **Untrusted runs** (>= 3 frames) are reported separately.

Why revised: in the first version (no untrusted class, no minimum length) the user saw that many "events" were
eyelid-point tracking errors with the eye open; of the 733 events, 213 had a negative opening and 183 lasted 1-2 frames.

Result of the rule on videos 0-12: 465 closure events (1-78 per video, 0.2-3.9 per minute, median duration 50-233 ms),
452 untrusted runs; 0.1-3.4% of the frames closed, 0.1-1.6% untrusted.

Seen while checking the new sample myself (a few items of videos 1 and 11, not the spot check): (1) when the eye closes
fully, the eyelid points scatter and the frames become *untrusted*, so the deepest part of a real blink shows up as an
untrusted run, not as a low opening (e.g. video 11, t = 1129.7 s: closure event = the closing phase, untrusted run = the
closed eye). (2) Some closure events with r = 0.45-0.7 show an eye that looks the same before, during and after (videos 1
and 11, which have narrow eyes); these may be false positives of the 0.70 threshold. Both are what the spot check has to
settle; the untrusted runs are therefore part of the sample.
